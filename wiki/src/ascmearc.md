# The Ascmearc

The Ascmearc, "the ash march", is the world you play in. Every new run starts there. **New run**
goes straight on to creating your character (see [Your character](character.md)), and the run
begins at eight in the morning on **Beorggeat**, the pass in the mountain wall on the march's
western edge: "The pass opens on the Ascmearc. Far to the east, Fyrbeorg smokes." Snow closes the
pass behind you. There is no way back, and nothing to explore there.

It is an old, plain-spoken land of farmers and barrow-diggers. A market town lives off the pass, a
cold barrow lies in the downs, a minster lies drowned in the fen, and a fire mountain smokes over
it all. How [travel](overworld.md#travel), [sight](sight.md#line-of-sight),
[locations](overworld.md#locations), and [local areas](overworld.md#local-areas) work on the
overworld is explained on [The overworld](overworld.md).

## Getting about

The road runs six cells east from the pass to **Cēaptūn**, the market town. It is under two hours'
walk, and in sight from the pass by day. From the town, roads lead on:

- east over the barrow downs of Hlāwdūn to the ford over Blæcwæter and the land beyond;
- south over the bridge on Beorhtburna and along its bank;
- north to the edge of the holly wood.

The march is about 120 cells across, three to four days by road. Rivers can be crossed only at the
bridge and the ford.

### Cēaptūn

**Cēaptūn** is the first place to go. It is a walled market town, and safe. On its square stand
two [shops](shops.md), a general store and a smithy, with private houses beside them; dogs and
cats wander its lanes; the Mynster, the temple, where you may
[rest](resting.md#how-resting-works) and Sister Hild [cures and mends](npcs.md#services) for
[gold](shops.md#money); the Gæsthūs, the inn, where Godgifu has news of the march; and the
Gildhalle of the [Weyfarers' Gilde](guild.md), where Wat One-Eye gives out work, with its
[yard of training dummies](guild.md#the-yard) across the lane (see
[Shops](shops.md) and [People and talk](npcs.md)).

You arrive on the square. The town has no [stairs](terrain.md#stairs). As in any outdoor area,
walk off its edge to return to its cell on the [overworld](overworld.md), or press `<` when no
enemy is about ([Leaving an area](overworld.md#leaving-an-area)).

### Holtwīc

At the end of the north road, some three hours' walk from the town on the edge of the holly wood,
lies **Holtwīc**, a farming village. It has cottages round a green and a well, barley fields
behind their hedges, and cottars who will pass the time of day. A dog and a cat live there, and
three hens scratch about in a fenced pen; its gate opens like a door. It is safe, and has no
[shop](shops.md).

The dogs and cats of both places and the village's hens are
[hounds](creatures.md#creature-hound), [cats](creatures.md#creature-cat), and
[chickens](creatures.md#creature-chicken). They are wildlife: they mean you no harm, and no quest
ever asks you to kill them.

Its wise-woman, **Gammer Wynflæd**, keeps a cottage apart under the first trees. She knows the
village, the wood, and the fair folk, and will [talk](npcs.md) of them. A girl of the village,
Ælfrun, has gone missing in the wood, and the wise-woman will ask you to bring her home: the first
[escort](quests.md#kinds-of-quests) of the march. Like the town, Holtwīc has no
[stairs](terrain.md#stairs); walk off its edge to leave.

### Drȳtorr

**Drȳtorr**, the wizard's tower, stands in a clearing at the western edge of the holly wood,
north-west of the fairy mound. It is a [hidden location](overworld.md#hidden-locations): Gammer
Wynflæd marks it on your map when she sends you there with her rowan, once the girl from the
village is home ([quests](quests.md#who-gives-quests)). **Master Ordgar**, the wizard, lives at
the top. He is a scholar, dry and exact, and he is friendly.

The tower has four floors, and you climb it by its [stairs](terrain.md#stairs). Its walls are
dressed stone that no pick, blast, or rod can break
([digging](digging.md#what-breaks-and-what-does-not)), and every floor has windows. A window is
an open gap in the wall: you can see, hear, and shoot through it, but not climb through it. From
the floors above, an arrow, a bolt, or anything you throw that comes down outside the tower falls
to the ground far below and is lost. Every floor's danger, traps, and folk are listed under
[the wizards' towers floor by floor](dungeons.md#the-wizards-towers-floor-by-floor).

- **The ground floor** is the inside of the tower in the clearing. It is big: three bands of
  rooms, one above the other. Through the windows you can see into the halls before you open
  the door. The wizard keeps it hard on callers. Two [hidden traps](traps.md#finding-traps) lie
  across the entrance hall. In the long gallery behind it, among the statues, two
  [gargoyles](creatures.md#creature-gargoyle) sit as still as stone until you pass beside them.
  In the north hall, three bays each hold a trap you can see, with something left behind it to
  tempt you: a potion, a scroll, and 25 gold. The stair up is in the middle of the north wall.
  The traps there hit as hard as on a [danger](dungeons.md#who-lives-there-and-how-many) 5
  floor. Now and then a sprite or a pixie of the fair folk comes out of the wood.
- **The second and third floors** are new each run: many rooms and halls, some behind doors,
  lit by wall torches, with hidden traps (arrow and spear traps, pits, and alarms; five to eight
  a floor). They are danger 6 and 7, and 8 to 14 of the tower's folk keep each one. These are the wizard's
  own making: [mannikins](creatures.md#creature-mannikin), his quick little men of clay, whose
  bite makes you drowsy and who open doors; [ward-stones](creatures.md#creature-ward-stone),
  which never move, slow whoever they look at, and spark at whoever comes beside them;
  gargoyles; and [loam men](creatures.md#creature-loam-man), slow and heavy guardians whose
  fists stagger. Besides them, a
  [lesser earth elemental](creatures.md#creature-lesser-earth-elemental) bound to the stones,
  and the wizard's household gone lively and hostile: books, brooms, candlesticks, chairs, coat
  stands, lanterns, and rugs ([animated objects](animated.md)). The tower's folk are found in
  the towers and nowhere else.
- **The top floor** is the wizard's study and his quarters, with no traps and nobody to fight.
  His braziers keep the rooms 12 °C warmer than the day outside
  ([hearth-warmed room](special-rooms.md#room-hearth)). Two doors lead out onto a balcony open to
  the sky, with a low wall round it. Rain falls on it, and it is as cold as the day.

From the floors above, the windows look out over the wood far below. You see the clearing and
whatever stands in it as it stood when you climbed: the land below does not move while you are
up there. You see it by day, dimly by moonlight, and not at all on a dark night. What you see
from up there is marked on your map of the clearing. A creature seen far below cannot be
attacked or targeted: look at it, and you are told only what it is.

You leave the clearing by walking off its edge, or by going up with `<` anywhere but on the
stair when nothing hostile is near.

### Wedertorr

**Wedertorr**, the weather tower, stands alone on the bare top of a down on Hlāwdūn, east of
Cealdhlāw. It is a [hidden location](overworld.md#hidden-locations): Master Ordgar marks it on
your map when he gives you [the wizards' quarrel](quests.md#the-wizards-quarrel). **Mistress
Ælfswīð**, the wizard, lives at the top. She calls storms, she is short with callers, and she is
friendly until the quarrel says otherwise. The first time you climb to her during the quarrel,
she offers you her side.

The tower has five floors, and it is much bigger than Drȳtorr. Its walls cannot be dug
([digging](digging.md#what-breaks-and-what-does-not)), and its windows are open gaps you can see,
hear, and shoot through, as at Drȳtorr.

- **The ground floor** is the inside of the tower on the down. Two
  [hidden traps](traps.md#finding-traps) lie across the south hall: a lightning trap and a frost
  trap. The way on is at the hall's east end. In the north hall, three bays in the wall each hold
  a trap you can see (lightning, frost, and a pit) with something behind it: a potion, a scroll,
  and 40 gold. A small room off the hall's west end has a scroll of lightning lying in it. The
  traps hit as hard as on a [danger](dungeons.md#who-lives-there-and-how-many) 7 floor. Now and
  then a will-o'-wisp or a barrow spider comes up onto the down.
- **The second, third, and fourth floors** are new each run and dark but for a torch here and
  there, so bring a light. They hold hidden traps (lightning and frost traps, pits, and alarms;
  five to eight a floor) and the wizard's household, six to ten a floor: her own
  [galvanic homunculi](creatures.md#creature-galvanic-homunculus) (little men of wax and copper
  whose touch jolts), [bound squalls](creatures.md#creature-bound-squall) (scraps of storm tied
  to the tower, which knock you off your feet and burst when they die), and
  [storm gargoyles](creatures.md#creature-storm-gargoyle) (slow stone wards with lightning in
  their claws); spark motes and lesser air elementals she has called; and bellows, kettles,
  lanterns, and books gone lively ([animated objects](animated.md)). None of them ever leaves the
  tower. The floors are danger 8, 9, and 10.
- **The top floor** is her workroom, her library, and her quarters, with no traps and nobody to
  fight. Her braziers keep the rooms warm, as at Drȳtorr. Two doors lead out onto a balcony
  that runs the whole width of the tower.

From the floors above, the windows look out over the down far below, as they do at Drȳtorr.

### Steorratorr

**Steorratorr**, the star tower, stands on the green plain of Ēastmearc where it runs down to the
grey sea. It is the tallest of the three towers. It is a
[hidden location](overworld.md#hidden-locations): Master Ordgar marks it on your map, with
Wedertorr, when he gives you [the wizards' quarrel](quests.md#the-wizards-quarrel). **Master
Sigeric**, the wizard, lives at the top. He makes wonders, he thinks very well of himself, and
he is friendly until the quarrel says otherwise. The first time you climb to him during the
quarrel, he offers you his side.

The tower has seven floors, as big as Wedertorr's. Its walls cannot be dug
([digging](digging.md#what-breaks-and-what-does-not)), and its windows are open gaps you can see,
hear, and shoot through, as at Drȳtorr.

- **The ground floor** is the inside of the tower on the plain. The south hall is a gallery of
  statues of Sigeric, and three [hidden traps](traps.md#finding-traps) lie across it: a teleport
  trap, a fireball trap, and a lightning trap. The way on is at the hall's west end. In the north
  hall, three bays in the wall each hold a trap you can see (lightning, frost, and a pit) with
  something behind it: a potion of greater healing, a scroll of haste, and 60 gold. A small room
  off the hall's west end has a scroll of fireball lying in it. The traps hit as hard as on a
  [danger](dungeons.md#who-lives-there-and-how-many) 9 floor. Now and then one of the drowned or a
  lesser water elemental walks up out of the sea.
- **The second to sixth floors** are new each run and dark but for a torch here and there, so
  bring a light. They hold hidden traps (fireball, lightning, and teleport traps, and pits; five
  to eight a floor) and the wizard's household, six to ten a floor: his own
  [brass peacocks](creatures.md#creature-brass-peacock) (whose peck can leave you
  [transfixed](status-list.md#status-transfixed), slow to act),
  [quicksilver hounds](creatures.md#creature-quicksilver-hound) (fast, with a poisonous bite),
  and [gilded likenesses](creatures.md#creature-gilded-likeness) (slow, heavy statues of himself
  that hit very hard); blades and suits of armour he has set walking; the imps and quasits he
  has bound; and teapots, cutlery, and books gone lively ([animated objects](animated.md)). None
  of them ever leaves the tower. The floors are danger 10 to 14.
- **The top floor** is his workshop, his quarters, and the landing at the top of the stair, with
  no traps and nobody to fight. His braziers keep the rooms warm, as at Drȳtorr. Two doors lead
  out onto a balcony down the whole east side of the tower, over the sea.

From the floors above, the windows look out over the plain and the sea far below.

### Līctūn

Four cells down the south road from the town is **Līctūn**, Cēaptūn's churchyard, where your
own dead are buried, and the crypts below it fill as they do. Its own page,
[Līctūn](lictun.md), tells it all.

### Gimmwīc

Far below the south-west corner, at the bottom of [Dēopgang](dungeon-guide.md#dēopgang), lies
**Gimmwīc**, the town of the **deep gnomes**. They are small, grey, bald folk of the deep who love
gemstones and making things more than anything else. They are friendly, and nothing comes there to
fight you. You cannot play one yet.

Their talk turns to their work, and they speak the Cornish of the deep: "my 'ansum", "dreckly"
(some day), "proper job" (well done). To them you are an *emmet*, someone from up top.

- **Headman Peran** speaks for the town in its moot hall. He will [talk](npcs.md) of the town, the
  surface, and the deep. Factor Wulfrun's letter for the deep gnomes goes to him
  ([quests](quests.md#who-gives-quests)), and he sends an answer back.
- **The Glimmering**, the temple, is lit by crystals that never go dark. Its floor is a
  [sanctuary](special-rooms.md): wounds heal faster there and nothing hostile steps in. **Mother
  Senara** [cures and mends](npcs.md#services) for gold.
- **Kerensa's Stones**, a gem dealer, sells and buys [gems](gems.md), rings, and amulets.
  Kerensa also [identifies](npcs.md#services) what you carry.
- **Jory's Outfitters** sells the makers' stock ([ingots](materials.md), billets, [herbs](herbs.md),
  and essences), tools, lanterns, food, and potions.
- **The cutting floor** has three [anvils](altars-forges-springs.md) and workbenches, free to use.
  **[Old Tregear](npcs.md#old-tregear)** works there, and teaches
  [Jewelcraft](skills.md#skill-jewelcraft) for gold.
- **Ysella the Binder** keeps to the garden at the town's east edge, where she binds the deep's
  crystal to the gnomes' work. Her [quest](quests.md#lures) opens the way into
  [Meyn Bew](dungeon-guide.md#meyn-bew), a pocket of the plane of earth, and asks you to lure its
  crystal guardian home. Bound, it stands in Kerensa's Stones for good, and every gem you
  [cut or set](gem-cutting.md) in the town comes out a little better.

Its stairs up lead back to Dēopgang's eleventh floor.

There is a quicker way home. The **Skeul Hir**, the gnomes' old long stair, climbs from a corner
of the arrival cave all the way up to the second floor of
[Lytelscræf](dungeons.md#the-dungeons), by the pass. Both ends look like plain floor until Headman
Peran gives you the moot's answer to carry up ([quests](quests.md#who-gives-quests)). Then he has
it opened, and it stays open both ways for good: stairs up in Gimmwīc, stairs down on Lytelscræf's
second floor. Nothing else ever uses it while it is shut, and searching will not find it.

### The dungeons

A little cave, **[Lytelscræf](dungeons.md#the-dungeons)**, opens two cells from the pass where you
come in. It has three short floors of rats, bats, and kobolds, with a chest at the bottom: the
easiest dungeon there is.

Three dungeons lie nearest the town, each deeper and more dangerous than the last (see
[Dungeons and danger](dungeons.md)):

- **[Cealdhlāw](dungeons.md#the-dungeons)**, the cold barrow in the downs, which you can see from
  the road;
- **[Fennmynster](dungeon-guide.md#fennmynster)**, the cellars of a minster drowned in the great
  fen;
- **[Fyrdelf](dungeon-guide.md#fyrdelf)**, the fire diggings under the hills below the volcano.

The last two are **[hidden](overworld.md#hidden-locations)**. Until a [quest](quests.md) tells you
where one lies, its cell is just the swamp or the hills it looks like (see
[Hidden locations](overworld.md#hidden-locations)). So are the
[four lairs](dungeon-guide.md#the-lairs-of-the-wanted) of the criminals the
[Weyfarers' Gilde](guild.md#the-wanted) wants dead: Wyrtholm in the fen, Wulfeshol in Deorcholt,
Bānfeld on the downs, and Brocenburg in the holly wood. You are shown only one of them.

Under the volcano, **[Ūtgang](dungeon-guide.md#ūtgang)**, a way up from the bottom of Fyrdelf, is
hidden too, and no one tells of it: you find its mouth by climbing out of it. Somewhere in the
holly wood near Holtwīc, a fairy mound hides **[Ælfhām](dungeon-guide.md#ælfhām)**, the hall of
the dark court of the fey.

In the south-west corner, at the foot of Grægweall, steps cut into the mountain lead down into
**[Dēopgang](dungeon-guide.md#dēopgang)**, the deepest and most dangerous dungeon of the march. It
is not hidden. At its bottom is [Gimmwīc](#gimmwīc), the deep gnomes' town.

The map below shows them all anyway. A few places lead "nowhere yet": `>` there says so.

## The names

Every name in the Ascmearc is a plain description of the place, put into Old English, so the name
tells you what you will find: a fen minster stands in the fen. The people have names of the same
kind. Creatures keep their plain English names.

The guild is younger than the town, so its names are Middle English, not Old. Its master, Wat
One-Eye, is a commoner, called by a short name and a byname. The guild's [ranks](guild.md#ranks)
(Tin to Gold) are plain English.

Most names write *ǣ* as **æ** and *ȳ* as **y** (Græg-, Fyr-).

The language has moved on since the old places were named. People of the march's own day go by
the later forms of the old names. The outlaws go by the bynames the broadsheets give them, in the
English of the day:

| Name | From | Register | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mother Sallow, the Fennhām Poisoner | *sallow*, the willow of the fens | a commoner's byname | a [wanted](guild.md#the-wanted) poisoner |
| Black Wystan, the Gentleman of the Road | Wystan, from *Wulfstān* "wolf stone" | the later form; a Victorian byname | a [wanted](guild.md#the-wanted) highwayman |
| Brother Osrick, the Resurrection Man | Osrick, from *Ōsrīc* "god ruler" | the later form; a Victorian byname | a [wanted](guild.md#the-wanted) grave robber |
| Sergeant Herrick, the Deserter | Herrick, from *Hererīc* "army ruler" | the later form; a Victorian byname | a [wanted](guild.md#the-wanted) deserter |
| [footpad](creatures.md#creature-footpad) | *foot* + *pad* "road" | Victorian, as the commons say it | a robber on foot |
