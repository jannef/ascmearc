# Races

Your race is the first choice of a new run (see [Your character](character.md)). It does several
things:

- It changes your starting [stats](stats.md).
- It gives you your eyes: the [vision type](vision.md#the-four-types) you start with.
- It sets how old you can be when you start.
- It adds to your [hit points](hit-points.md#hit-points).
- It trains you in a [skill](skills.md) or two. [Humans](races.md#race-human) instead get a little
  more in one of their class's skills (see [Skills](skills.md#which-skills-you-start-with)).
- It may give you a rank in a [weapon skill](weapon-skills.md) or two, on top of your class's.
- It adds a few things to your [starting kit](starting-kit.md): an [elf](races.md#race-elf)'s
  waybread, a [dwarf](races.md#race-dwarf)'s pick-axe, a [halfling](races.md#race-halfling)'s pipe. It also gives the everyday clothes and shoes of
  your folk where your class gives none ([halflings](races.md#race-halfling) go barefoot).
- It may close a few [classes](classes.md) to you: no drow paladins, no elven witches (see
  [Who may take which](classes.md#who-may-take-which)).

The first table is what each race adds to your starting stats. The second gives its eyes, its
starting ages, its hit points, and its weapon skills. A short description of each race follows.

The changes are added to the average score of 10, together with your gender's, your
[class](classes.md)'s, and your [birth sign's](horoscope.md#what-a-sign-does). Most are plain
numbers. A few are dice, rolled when you start: `1d2+1` is one or two, plus one (see
[How your stats add up](character.md#how-your-stats-add-up)). Your starting age is picked at random
within the race's range.

Races are not balanced against each other. [Humans](races.md#race-human) start with no gifts and no
flaws. The others trade one strength for one weakness, or for two.

[Halflings](races.md#race-halfling), [gnomes](races.md#race-gnome), [goblins](races.md#race-goblin),
[kobolds](races.md#race-kobold), and [ratfolk](races.md#race-ratfolk) are small, and carry three
quarters of what the others do. [Dwarves](races.md#race-dwarf) and [duergar](races.md#race-duergar)
are not slowed or hindered by a load until they strain under it. See [Burden](burden.md).

Races need different amounts of [food](food.md). [Elves](races.md#race-elf),
[drow](races.md#race-drow), [goblins](races.md#race-goblin), and [kobolds](races.md#race-kobold)
need the least, [halflings](races.md#race-halfling) (second breakfast) the most. See
[Hunger](hunger.md).

## How races grow

Some races grow further than others.

- **[Humans](races.md#race-human)** have the most room to grow. Each
  [potential](stats.md#potentials) is half as much again, 24 or 26 in every stat. And every
  [training mark](stats.md#raising-your-stats) they earn, a stat's or a
  [skill's](skills.md#improving-a-skill), has a one in four chance to count twice.
- **[Half-orcs](races.md#race-half-orc)** grow far in body. Their Strength, Dexterity, and
  Toughness potentials are two and a half times as much: 40 (the most there is), 38, and 40. Their
  marks count twice one time in four, like a human's.
- **[Half-elves](races.md#race-half-elf)** grow far in mind. Their Intelligence, Willpower, and
  Presence potentials are half as much again.

Every other race grows as the plain rules say. A potential is multiplied after your race's
adjustment is added, and rounded to the nearest whole number (a half rounds up). No potential goes
past 40.

Only [halflings](races.md#race-halfling) smoke a pipe. On a full belly, an hour's smoke trains their
stats as it empties the belly, and only halflings carve pipes. See [Your pipe](pipe.md).

## The evil races

Six races start on the evil side of [alignment](alignment.md).
[Goblins](races.md#race-goblin), [kobolds](races.md#race-kobold),
[lizardfolk](races.md#race-lizardfolk), and [ratfolk](races.md#race-ratfolk) start Callous;
[drow](races.md#race-drow) and [duergar](races.md#race-duergar) start Evil. Some are poor choices
on purpose, and their [levels](experience.md#levels) come quicker for it (see
[Experience](experience.md)).

- **[Goblins](races.md#race-goblin)** are the quick, crappy choice. They are weak and frail but
  quick-handed and sneaky ([Stealth](skills.md#skill-stealth)), short-lived, and light eaters. Only
  the kobolds' [levels](experience.md#levels) come faster.
- **[Drow](races.md#race-drow)** are the [elves](races.md#race-elf) of the deep dark: graceful and
  sharp, but frail. They are a little [resistant](damage-types.md#armour-and-resistance) to
  [poison](damage-types.md#damage-poison), and like elves they
  [notice every hidden thing in passing](searching.md#noticing-in-passing). Strong light dazzles
  them (below). They are long-lived, so their [levels](experience.md#levels) come slowly.
- **[Kobolds](races.md#race-kobold)** are the weakest of all, but good with
  [traps](traps.md#finding-traps). They [notice hidden traps in passing](searching.md#noticing-in-passing)
  (never a [secret door](doors.md#secret-doors)), find hidden things more easily, and start trained
  in [Disable device](skills.md#skill-disable-device). They also know
  [Trapwright](class-craftsperson.md#talent-trapwright) from the start, whatever their class, and can
  [rig traps](traps.md#rigging-traps). With their huge families, their
  [levels](experience.md#levels) come cheapest of all.
- **[Lizardfolk](races.md#race-lizardfolk)** are strong and tough, dull and alien. Their scales soak
  a point of every physical blow, as natural armour does for a [creature](creatures.md)'s hide (see
  [Combat](combat.md#protection)). They are
  [cold-blooded](temperature.md#cold-blooded): slower in the cold and quicker in the heat. They wade
  [water](terrain.md#water) as quickly and as quietly as they walk, and they start far better at
  [Swim](skills.md#skill-swim) than anyone else.
- **[Duergar](races.md#race-duergar)**, the [dwarves](races.md#race-dwarf)' grey cousins, are tough
  and dour. Like dwarves, they shrug off a [load](burden.md) until they strain under it, and they
  [notice what is hidden](searching.md#noticing-in-passing) in stone.
- **[Ratfolk](races.md#race-ratfolk)** are nimble scavengers, weak and shunned. They are hard to
  sicken, with a bonus on every [check](diseases.md#checks) against a [disease](diseases.md), and
  they eat rotten food without harm (see [Food](food.md)). Eating those who think wakes [their
  craving](food.md#the-ratfolks-craving), which makes them stronger and hungrier.

No [creature](creatures.md) takes your side because of your race.
[Hostile](creatures.md#factions) [kobolds](races.md#race-kobold) attack a kobold too. Good folk
greet an Evil character coldly, but that comes from your [alignment](alignment.md), not your race.

**Strong [light](light.md#what-light-is)** dazzles [drow](races.md#race-drow). Light is strong
enough when the [sun](sun.md#sky-light) is well up under a clear sky, or a bright fire is close
by; your own [lantern](lantern.md) alone is not enough. While the light on your own cell is that
bright, you see nothing farther than ten cells and fight a little worse. The game tells you when it
begins and ends, and the [status bar](the-screen.md#the-screen) shows `Dazzled` while it lasts.

## Racial magic

Three races start knowing a little magic, whatever their class:

- **[Gnomes](races.md#race-gnome)** know the [Glamour](magic.md#spell-glamour) spell, an
  [illusion](schools.md#school-illusion).
- **[Drow](races.md#race-drow)** know the [Fume](magic.md#spell-fume) spell.
- **[Duergar](races.md#race-duergar)** can fade from sight, as their grey kin in the deep do:
  [Invisibility](abilities.md#ability-fade).

The two spells work as any spell you know. You cast them from the
[abilities menu](abilities.md#the-abilities-menu) and pay their [mana](mana.md#spending-it) in full,
and reading their grimoire deepens them ([Spell ranks](learning-spells.md#spell-ranks)).

Invisibility is in the menu's Racial group. It takes no time and no mana, but it is heard, as a
chant is. You are [Invisible](status-list.md#status-invisible) for two minutes: no creature that
needs eyes sees you, though they still hear you. Then you cannot fade again for five minutes from
the moment you faded, and the status bar shows `Invisibility spent` while you wait. While you are
[Silenced](status-list.md#status-silenced) you cannot fade at all.

Gnomes may also pick *Gnomish illusionist* (below), which makes illusion spells cost a quarter less
mana.

## Racial talents

Each race except the humans has [talents](talents-origins.md#racial-talents) that only it may pick, in the
Racial tree. Humans get Versatile instead: one more talent at creation and at level 30.
Dwarves also get Old grudges, a free Sworn foe now and then (below).
Halflings are also given Second breakfast: Bloated never slows them, they hold twice as much food,
and a full belly slowly trains their stats (see [Hunger](hunger.md#a-halflings-second-breakfast)).

## The elves' ways

[Elves](races.md#race-elf) and [half-elves](races.md#race-half-elf) may take four narrow
**ways**. Each makes one way of fighting much better, and every other way of the same kind
worse. The table above has the numbers.

- **[Way of the longbow](talents-origins.md#talent-way-of-the-longbow)**: shots with a long war
  bow are better. Every other shot (a short bow, a composite bow, a crossbow, a sling) and every
  [throw](ranged.md#shooting-and-throwing) is worse. Blows are untouched.
- **[Way of the long blade](talents-origins.md#talent-way-of-the-long-blade)**: blows with a
  weapon of [Long blades](weapon-skills.md#weapon-skill-long-blades) are better. Every other blow
  is worse: other weapons, bare hands, and a shield's bash. Kicks, shots, and throws are untouched.
- **[Way of the duel](talents-origins.md#talent-way-of-the-duel)**: blows in the
  [Duelist](fighting-styles.md#style-duelist) style (one weapon, the other hand empty) are better.
  Blows in any other style are worse: with a shield, with two weapons, with a two-handed weapon, or
  with bare hands.
- **[Way of far casting](talents-origins.md#talent-way-of-far-casting)**: spells that roll to hit
  are better when cast as a [Sniper](schools.md#style-sniper), from two or more cells away. Cast
  as a [Toucher](schools.md#style-toucher) (at a creature beside you) or as a
  [Battle caster](schools.md#style-battle-caster) (in armour that hinders casting), they are worse.
  Spells that never roll to hit are untouched.

The ways add up with each other. Way of the long blade and Way of the duel together make a lone
long sword better still, and a sword with a shield worse twice over.

## The dwarves' oaths

A dwarf never forgets a wrong. Every dwarf gets a free
[Sworn foe](talents-fighting.md#talent-sworn-foe) when they are made, and another at levels 10, 20,
30, 40, and 50. Each time, you choose the kind of creature to swear against, just as when you pick
Sworn foe yourself. These come on top of your other [talents](talents.md#when-you-get-a-talent):
at creation the Talents step asks for the free one first, and at those levels the
[level-up screen](experience.md#levelling-up) asks for it after the skill rolls. You can still pick
Sworn foe with an ordinary talent for more kinds.

**[Slayer's Oath](talents-origins.md#talent-slayers-oath)** is the dwarves' own follow-up. You can take it for a kind you are already sworn
against, and only for that kind. It makes your attacks against that kind surer and deadlier still
(+1 to hit and +3 damage, on top of Sworn foe's +1 and +2). From then on, every creature of that
kind you kill yourself is an **oath kill**. A kill made by an [ally](allies.md) does not count.

Your oath kills are one running total across all your oaths. A creature that belongs to two kinds
you have oaths against still counts once. The [character screen](character.md) shows the total as
`Oath kills` once you have an oath.

As the total grows, it passes the marks in the table below. At each one the game says what you feel,
and you gain a lasting talent, listed on your Talents page as `oath`. Each comes once, and they add
up: a dwarf with 300 oath kills has the first five.

{{#include generated/oaths.md}}

The bonuses "against every kind of creature you are sworn against" count once per attack, even
when a creature belongs to several kinds you are sworn against. They come on top of what each Sworn
foe and Slayer's Oath gives. A dwarf with all ten marks has +4 to hit and +8 damage from the table
against those kinds alone. [Doom of their kind](#talent-oath-doom) saves your life once. If you also have another way
to escape death once, such as the witch's [Hag's bargain](class-witch.md#talent-hags-bargain), each one works once.

**How far is that?** Reaching level 20 takes about 1,700 kills of creatures of your own level, and
level 50 about 7,400 (see [Experience](experience.md#levels)). A dwarf needs 15% more, and real
counts run higher still, since weaker monsters are worth less. If you swear oaths against kinds
you meet often, roughly a quarter of your kills are oath kills. That puts the 500 mark near level
20, 1,200 near level 30, and the last mark, 2,500, near level 50.
